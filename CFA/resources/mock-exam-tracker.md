# Mock Exam Tracker

## The protocol

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Full length, timed** | 180 questions, two 135-minute sessions, one day |
| **Real conditions** | No notes, no phone, a small erasable board only |
| **Same time of day** | As your scheduled exam |
| **Review before the next one** | Never sit two mocks in a row unreviewed |
| **Budget 2–3 hours for the review** | Longer than you expect. This is where mocks actually work |

**What the dominant category means:**

| Dominant | Diagnosis | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **K** | Coverage is incomplete | Back to the specific modules — but only the LOS you missed, not the whole topic |
| **A** | You know it but not fluently | **Volume of questions.** This is the most common pattern at mock 1 |
| **C** | Your **process** is the problem, not your knowledge | Slow down on the stem, write out units, check signs, clear the calculator. **Cheapest category to fix, most often ignored** |
| **T** | Pacing | You are over-investing in hard questions. Practise flag-and-move |

## Pacing check

```
180 questions ÷ 270 minutes = 90 seconds per question
Target: 90 questions in 130 minutes, leaving 5 minutes for flagged items
```

## After each mock — the three questions

1. **Which topic cost me the most marks, weighted by exam weight?**
2. **Which error category dominated, and what specific change follows from it?**
3. **Did I run out of time, and if so, where did the time go?**

Write the answers down. A mock reviewed properly is worth more than three mocks sat and filed.
