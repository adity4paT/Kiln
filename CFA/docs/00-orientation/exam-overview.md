# The Level I Exam — What You Are Actually Being Tested On

## Format

| Attribute | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Sessions | 2, taken on the same day |
| Questions | 180 total — 90 per session |
| Time | 135 minutes per session (4.5 hours total testing) |
| Pace | **90 seconds per question**, average |
| Question type | Multiple choice, three options (A/B/C) — no negative marking |
| Delivery | Computer-based, at a test centre |
| Calculators | Texas Instruments BA II Plus (incl. Professional) or HP 12C only |

Session 1 and Session 2 each cover a mix of topics; you cannot go back to Session 1 after it closes.

**Read this table strategically.** Ethics + FSA + Equity + Fixed Income together are roughly
48–62% of the exam. That is where the marginal hour pays best, and it is exactly why the study
plan front-loads FSA and gives Ethics its own parallel track.

## No negative marking — and what follows from it

Never leave a question blank. A pure guess is worth 1/3 of a mark in expectation; a blank is worth
zero. Two consequences for exam-day technique:

1. **Flag and move.** If you are 30 seconds in with no route to an answer, pick your best guess,
   flag it, and move. Coming back with fresh eyes is cheaper than grinding.
2. **Eliminate before you guess.** Killing one option lifts a guess from 33% to 50%. On Level I,
   one option is very often eliminable on units, sign, or magnitude alone.

## How the pass mark works

CFA Institute does not publish a fixed pass mark. The minimum passing score is set by the Board
using a standard-setting process, and historical pass rates have generally sat in the ~35–45% band
for Level I. Practical implications:

- **You are not competing against a fixed 70%.** You are being placed against a standard of minimal
  competence. Broad adequacy across all topics beats brilliance in three and blanks in two.
- **Ethics is the designated tie-breaker.** Candidates whose total score is near the minimum
  passing score have their Ethics performance used to decide the outcome. This is the single
  strongest argument for the 150-hour parallel Ethics track in this plan.
- **Results are reported as bands/quartiles per topic**, so you see relative strength per topic but
  not a raw score.

## The command verbs, and what they oblige you to do

Every LOS opens with a verb. The verb tells you the depth of processing required — and therefore
how you should study that module. Treat this as a contract.

| Verb | What the exam can ask | How to study it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **define / identify / list** | Recall a term or a member of a set | Flashcard. Pure recall. |
| **describe** | State features, steps, or characteristics | Be able to write 3–5 bullets from memory |
| **explain** | Give the mechanism or the "why" | Be able to teach it in two sentences |
| **compare / contrast** | Put two things side by side | Build a two-column table; memorise the *differences*, not the items |
| **calculate** | Produce a number | Do it on the calculator, timed, until it's automatic |
| **interpret** | Say what a number means | Always pair with *calculate* — know the direction of every input |
| **demonstrate / determine** | Apply to a fresh scenario | Practice with unfamiliar question stems, not worked examples |
| **evaluate / analyze** | Judge quality or reach a conclusion | Highest bar — expect multi-step vignette-style items |

A useful filter: if the LOS verb is *calculate*, a flashcard is not enough. If the verb is
*describe*, doing twenty calculations is a waste of an evening.

## What Level I is not

- It is not a memory test of the entire curriculum text. It is a test of the LOS.
- It is not mathematically deep. The hard part is *volume and breadth*, not difficulty.
- It is not forgiving of pacing failure. Most candidates who run out of time lost it in the first
  40 questions, over-investing in items they could have flagged.

## Exam-day mechanics worth rehearsing

- Bring a valid international travel passport. It is the only accepted ID.
- Test-centre rules prohibit notes, phones, and anything in your pockets. Your calculator cover and
  a clear water bottle are typically fine; check the current Candidate Bulletin.
- You get a scratch surface (usually an erasable board). **Practice using one** — do at least three
  mocks with no paper, only a small board. It changes how you lay out a time line.
- Budget 5 minutes at the end of each session for flagged questions. That means running the first
  90 questions in 130 minutes, not 135.
