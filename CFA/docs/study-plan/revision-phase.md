# The Revision Phase

*After day 150 — or carved out of it. Read the honest caveat below before deciding which.*

## Where the revision phase comes from

The 150-day plan covers **first-pass coverage of all 102 modules**. Retrieval (Pass 2) and the
weekly mixed sets are built into the four core hours, but a full **revision-and-mocks phase is not
inside the 150 days**.

You therefore have two options, and the choice must be made in **Month 1**, not in week 20:

| If your exam is… | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| **~200 days away** | Run the plan at **4 hours/day** (3 core + 1 Ethics) and add this **6-week revision phase at the end**. This is the comfortable version, and the one to choose if you can |
| **Exactly 150 days away** | **Compress the low-weight blocks** to buy revision time: take 3 days from Derivatives, 3 from Alternatives, and 2 from Economics to free **8 revision days**. This is a real trade — a shallower first pass on 13–27 questions' worth of material — not a free one |

## The six-week revision phase

| Week | Focus | Mock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Weak areas.** Work only what is in `trackers/weak-areas.md`, hardest first | Mock #2 at week end |
| **2** | **Heavy topics.** FSA, Equity, Fixed Income, Ethics — 62% of the exam between them | — |
| **3** | **Calculation drills.** Every formula on the formula sheet, timed, on the calculator | Mock #3 at week end |
| **4** | **Light topics + Ethics scenarios.** Derivatives, Economics, Corporate Issuers, Alternatives | — |
| **5** | **Full mixed practice.** Timed sets across all topics, 90 questions at a sitting | Mock #4 at week end |
| **6** | **Taper.** Formula sheet, Ethics, weak areas. **Nothing new** | Mock #5 early in the week |

## The mock exam protocol

**Mocks are the highest-value activity in the revision phase, and most candidates waste them by
not reviewing properly.**

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Full length, timed** | 180 questions, two 135-minute sessions, one day |
| **Real conditions** | No notes, no phone, a small erasable board only, at the time of day your exam is scheduled |
| **Review before the next one** | A mock you have not reviewed teaches you nothing. Never sit two in a row unreviewed |
| **Budget 2–3 hours for the review** | Longer than most candidates expect, and the reason mocks work |

**For every wrong answer, categorise it — this is the whole point:**

| Code | Meaning | The fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **K** | **Knowledge gap** — didn't know it | Back to the module |
| **A** | **Application error** — knew it, misapplied it | More questions on that LOS |
| **C** | **Careless** — misread the stem, wrong sign, wrong units | A **process** fix, not a study fix |
| **T** | **Time** — guessed because the clock ran out | A **pacing** fix |

> **These four have four completely different remedies.** Lumping them together as "I got it wrong"
> is how candidates re-study material they already know while never fixing their actual problem.
> Record the code in [`../../resources/mock-exam-tracker.md`](../../resources/mock-exam-tracker.md).

**What the categories usually mean in aggregate:**

- **Mostly K** → coverage is incomplete. Go back to the modules, but only the specific LOS
- **Mostly A** → you know the material but not fluently. Volume of questions is the fix
- **Mostly C** → your process is the problem, not your knowledge. Slow down on the stem, write out
  units, check signs. This is the **cheapest** category to fix and the most often ignored
- **Mostly T** → pacing. You are over-investing in hard questions. Practise flagging and moving

## Pacing

```
180 questions ÷ 270 minutes = 90 seconds per question

Target: finish the first 90 questions in 130 minutes, leaving 5 minutes for flagged items
```

**The flag-and-move rule:** if you are 30 seconds in with no route to an answer, **pick your best
guess, flag it, and move**. Coming back with fresh eyes is cheaper than grinding — and there is no
negative marking, so a blank is strictly worse than a guess.

**Where candidates lose time:** almost always in the **first 40 questions**, over-investing in items
they could have flagged. Track your split at the 45-question mark in every mock.

## The final week

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Review the **formula sheet** daily | Start any new topic |
| Work **Ethics scenarios** | Sit a full mock in the last two days |
| Clear remaining **weak-area** entries | Cram — it displaces sleep, which costs more |
| **Sleep properly** | Change your calculator or your process |
| Confirm **logistics**: passport, test centre location, arrival time | |
| Check the **calculator**: fresh batteries, decimals at 9, P/Y at 1, BGN off | |

## Exam day

**Before:**
- **Passport** — the only accepted ID
- Calculator with **fresh batteries**, plus a permitted spare
- Arrive early; know the route and the parking

**During:**
- **Answer every question.** No negative marking means a blank is strictly worse than a guess
- **Eliminate before guessing** — killing one option lifts a guess from 33% to 50%
- **Flag and move** at 30 seconds with no route
- **Budget 5 minutes** at the end of each session for flagged items
- Between sessions: **do not discuss the first session** with anyone. It changes nothing and
  damages your second-session focus

**After:** results are reported as **bands or quartiles per topic**, not as a raw score. You will see
relative strength by topic but not a number.
