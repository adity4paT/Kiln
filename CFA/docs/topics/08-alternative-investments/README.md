## Why this topic sits here

Alternative Investments is studied **eighth**, in the second half of Month 4, immediately after
Derivatives. The placement is deliberate: **LM5 (natural resources) needs the contango and
backwardation material from Derivatives LM4**, and hedge fund strategies in LM6 are described in
terms of options and short positions.

It also draws on almost everything before it — private debt analysis is **Fixed Income LM16**,
private equity valuation is **Equity LM6 and LM7**, the fee and return measurement work is **QM LM3**,
and the correlation caveats that run through every module come from **Portfolio Management LM1**.

At **7–10%** it is a mid-weight topic with a generous 60 hours, and it is unusually **readable** —
largely descriptive, with a small number of calculations (fees, multiples, cap rates, and the
commodity return decomposition) that recur reliably.

**The single idea that unifies the topic:** alternative investments are reported through
**appraisal-based, voluntarily disclosed, survivorship-filtered** data. Almost every apparent
advantage — low volatility, low correlation, high Sharpe ratios — is **partly a measurement
artefact**. An analyst who can say which parts are real and which are not has understood this topic.

> **LM2 is the module that makes the rest honest.** The biases and measurement problems it
> establishes — survivorship, backfill, self-selection, smoothing — recur in every subsequent module.
> Study it properly and the later modules largely write themselves.

## The formulas this topic lives on

Five things from this topic must be automatic.

```
1. Net return after a full fee sequence: management fee → hurdle (hard/soft) → high water mark
                                          → performance fee
2. TVPI = DPI + RVPI;  and the DPI/RVPI split is the quality test
3. Value = NOI / Cap rate     — a LOWER cap rate means a HIGHER value
4. Commodity total return = SPOT + ROLL + COLLATERAL
     Contango → NEGATIVE roll.  Backwardation → POSITIVE roll.
5. LBO return = operational improvement + DEBT PAYDOWN + multiple expansion
```

And one sentence that should accompany every reported alternatives statistic:
**appraisal-based valuation smooths returns, understating both volatility and correlation.**
