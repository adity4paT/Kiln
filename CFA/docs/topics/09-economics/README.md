## Why this topic sits here

Economics is studied **ninth**, at the start of Month 5, and the placement is deliberate.

Economics is **broad, largely descriptive, and only 6–9% of the exam**. Most of its LOS use the
verbs *describe* and *explain*, which reward **recency** rather than depth of practice. Studied in
Month 1 it would be substantially forgotten by exam day; studied in Month 5 it is fresh.

It also has the **fewest prerequisites** of any topic — which is precisely why it can be moved late
without cost. The one calculation-heavy module, **LM8 (Exchange Rate Calculations)**, needs the
no-arbitrage material from **QM LM4** and **Derivatives LM4**, both long since covered.

The connections run mostly **outward**: LM4's monetary policy underlies the yield curve in **Fixed
Income LM9**; LM7's balance of payments underlies sovereign credit in **Fixed Income LM15**; LM1's
market structures are **Equity LM9's** five forces from the economics side.

At 40 hours for 8 modules this is the lightest per-module allocation in the plan, and appropriately
so — the material is readable and the calculations are few.

> **LM8 is the exception to everything above.** It is the only heavily calculational module in the
> topic, and cross-rates and forward points are among the most reliably tested calculations on the
> exam. Give it the full 5 hours even if the descriptive modules go faster than budgeted.

## The formulas this topic lives on

Four things from this topic must be automatic.

```
1. Breakeven: P = ATC.  Shutdown: P < AVC.  (Short run vs long run — sunk costs.)
2. HHI = Σ(share as %)² — and it ignores barriers to entry, which matter more
3. Real exchange rate = Nominal × (CPI_base / CPI_price)
4. F(P/B) = S(P/B) × (1 + r_price) / (1 + r_base)    — PRICE currency's rate on TOP
```

Plus three relationships that decide questions on their own:
- **The higher-interest-rate currency trades at a FORWARD DISCOUNT** (LM8)
- **The impossible trinity:** fixed rate + free capital flows + independent monetary policy — **pick two** (LM4, LM7)
- **Indicator classification by reversibility:** forward-looking and reversible → leading; committed → coincident; a consequence → lagging (LM2)

## Topic wrap-up

Before you tick this topic complete and move to the final QM block:

- [ ] All 8 modules ticked, with their **Done when** lists genuinely cleared
- [ ] I can identify a market structure from a description and apply the shutdown rule
- [ ] I can classify an unfamiliar economic indicator using the reversibility logic
- [ ] I can judge a fiscal stance from the change in the **structural** deficit
- [ ] I can describe all five monetary transmission channels and state the impossible trinity
- [ ] I can list the five levels of economic integration in order
- [ ] I can compute a cross-rate reliably, by writing the quotes down as fractions
- [ ] I can compute a forward rate from points with the correct scaling, for a JPY pair and a non-JPY pair
- [ ] I have completed **50+ Economics questions**, logged in `trackers/weak-areas.md`

**Day 130 checkpoint.** Twenty days of new material remain — the second Quantitative Methods block,
which contains the two hardest modules in the curriculum. Economics is the last descriptive topic;
from here to day 150 the work is entirely quantitative.
