## Core concepts

### Who is covered

The Code and Standards apply to **CFA Institute members** and to **CFA Program candidates**.
Candidates are bound from the moment they register, so a candidate who breaches the Standards is
subject to sanction **before ever becoming a member**.

### The Professional Conduct Program

**Enforcement is carried out by the CFA Institute Board of Governors, through the Disciplinary
Review Committee (DRC), with investigations conducted by Professional Conduct staff.**

**Sources of an inquiry:**

| Source | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Self-disclosure** | On the **annual Professional Conduct Statement**, members and candidates disclose any involvement in civil litigation, criminal investigation, or matters that reflect on their professional conduct |
| **Written complaints** | From clients, employers, the public, or other members |
| **Public sources** | Media reports of misconduct |
| **CFA Program conduct** | Exam proctor reports of testing violations |
| **Analysis of exam materials** | Monitoring for irregularities |

**The process:**

1. **Inquiry** — Professional Conduct staff investigate, request a written explanation from the
   member or candidate, may interview them and others, and collect documents
2. **Conclusion** — staff may:
   - Find **no disciplinary sanction** is appropriate
   - Issue a **cautionary letter**
   - **Discipline** the member or candidate
3. If discipline is proposed, the member/candidate may **accept** the proposed sanction or **reject**
   it and request a **hearing before a DRC panel**
4. **Sanctions** range from a private censure to **revocation of membership and of the right to use
   the CFA designation**, and for candidates, **suspension or prohibition from the CFA Program**

> **Note what CFA Institute can and cannot do:** it can **revoke membership and the designation**
> and bar someone from the programme. It **cannot** impose fines or imprisonment — those are matters
> for regulators and courts. The sanction is **professional**, and its force comes from the
> designation's value.

### The Code of Ethics — six components

**Members and Candidates must:**

1. **Act with integrity, competence, diligence, respect, and in an ethical manner** with the public,
   clients, prospective clients, employers, employees, colleagues in the investment profession, and
   other participants in the global capital markets.
2. **Place the integrity of the investment profession and the interests of clients above their own
   personal interests.**
3. **Use reasonable care and exercise independent professional judgment** when conducting investment
   analysis, making investment recommendations, taking investment actions, and engaging in other
   professional activities.
4. **Practise and encourage others to practise in a professional and ethical manner** that will
   reflect credit on themselves and the profession.
5. **Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets** for the ultimate benefit of
   society.
6. **Maintain and improve their professional competence** and strive to maintain and improve the
   competence of other investment professionals.

> **Component 2 is the hierarchy from LM1**: the **integrity of the profession** and the **interests
> of clients** come **above the member's own interests**.

### The seven Standards of Professional Conduct

**This structure is the map for the next eight modules. Memorise it completely — the Roman numeral
and letter, and what each covers.**

| Standard | Sub-sections |
| --- | --- |
| **I. PROFESSIONALISM** | **A.** Knowledge of the Law · **B.** Independence and Objectivity · **C.** Misrepresentation · **D.** Misconduct |
| **II. INTEGRITY OF CAPITAL MARKETS** | **A.** Material Nonpublic Information · **B.** Market Manipulation |
| **III. DUTIES TO CLIENTS** | **A.** Loyalty, Prudence, and Care · **B.** Fair Dealing · **C.** Suitability · **D.** Performance Presentation · **E.** Preservation of Confidentiality |
| **IV. DUTIES TO EMPLOYERS** | **A.** Loyalty · **B.** Additional Compensation Arrangements · **C.** Responsibilities of Supervisors |
| **V. INVESTMENT ANALYSIS, RECOMMENDATIONS, AND ACTIONS** | **A.** Diligence and Reasonable Basis · **B.** Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients · **C.** Record Retention |
| **VI. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST** | **A.** Disclosure of Conflicts · **B.** Priority of Transactions · **C.** Referral Fees |
| **VII. RESPONSIBILITIES AS A CFA INSTITUTE MEMBER OR CFA CANDIDATE** | **A.** Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs · **B.** Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program |

**A mnemonic for the seven Standards:**

```
I    — PROFESSIONALISM                (how you conduct yourself)
II   — INTEGRITY OF CAPITAL MARKETS   (duties to the MARKET)
III  — DUTIES TO CLIENTS              (duties to the CLIENT)
IV   — DUTIES TO EMPLOYERS            (duties to the EMPLOYER)
V    — INVESTMENT ANALYSIS...         (duties in the WORK ITSELF)
VI   — CONFLICTS OF INTEREST          (duties when interests collide)
VII  — RESPONSIBILITIES AS A MEMBER   (duties to CFA INSTITUTE)
```

> **The count to memorise: 4 · 2 · 5 · 3 · 3 · 3 · 2 sub-sections.** Being able to say how many
> sub-sections each Standard has, and name them, is a reliable check that the structure is genuinely
> learned rather than vaguely familiar.

### How to answer an Ethics question

**The method, applied to every scenario:**

| Step | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | **Who are the stakeholders**, and what duty is owed to each? |
| **2** | **Which Standard is engaged?** Often more than one |
| **3** | **What does that Standard require** — disclosure, abstention, prior written consent, a reasonable basis, a record? |
| **4** | **Does the described conduct meet that requirement?** |

**The recurring remedies.** Most Standards resolve into one of a small set of required actions, and
recognising which applies is most of the work:

| Remedy | Where it appears |
| --- | --- |
| **Disclose** | Conflicts (VI-A), referral fees (VI-C), the process behind a recommendation (V-B) |
| **Obtain prior written consent** | Additional compensation (IV-B), outside activities that compete with the employer (IV-A) |
| **Abstain / do not act** | Material non-public information (II-A), market manipulation (II-B) |
| **Treat fairly** | Fair dealing among clients (III-B) |
| **Have a reasonable basis** | Diligence (V-A) |
| **Keep records** | Record retention (V-C) |
| **Client first** | Loyalty, prudence, care (III-A); priority of transactions (VI-B) |

> **Two answers that are almost never correct on the exam:**
>
> **"Do nothing"** — if a scenario describes a conflict or a concern, the Standards almost always
> require some action: disclosure, consent, abstention, or escalation.
>
> **"It was legal"** — the Code sets a higher bar than the law (LM1).
>
> Conversely, **disclosure alone is often insufficient**. Some situations require abstention
> (material non-public information cannot be cured by disclosing that you have it) or **prior
> written consent** (additional compensation). Identify which remedy the Standard actually specifies.

## Exam traps

> **Trap 1 — Thinking candidates are not bound.** **Candidates are subject to the Code and Standards
> from registration**, before ever becoming members.

> **Trap 2 — Overstating CFA Institute's powers.** It can **revoke membership and the designation**
> and bar someone from the programme. It **cannot fine or imprison**.

> **Trap 3 — Not knowing the structure cold.** You must be able to name the **Standard, the
> sub-section letter, and what it covers**. Vague familiarity is not enough for scenario questions.

> **Trap 4 — Choosing "do nothing".** If a scenario describes a conflict or concern, the Standards
> almost always require **disclosure, consent, abstention, or escalation**.

> **Trap 5 — Assuming disclosure always cures.** It does **not**. **Material non-public information**
> requires **abstention**; **additional compensation** requires **prior written consent**. Identify
> the specific remedy.

> **Trap 6 — Missing that multiple Standards can apply.** A single scenario frequently engages two
> or three. Exam questions often ask which is **least likely** to be violated.

> **Trap 7 — Confusing the Code with the Standards.** The **Code has six components** (principles);
> the **Standards have seven Roman numerals** (testable rules).

**2.** Describe the Professional Conduct Program's enforcement process and the sanctions available.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Structure:** the **CFA Institute Board of Governors** has overall responsibility, exercised through the **Disciplinary Review Committee (DRC)**. **Professional Conduct staff** conduct investigations.

**How an inquiry starts:**
- **Self-disclosure** on the annual **Professional Conduct Statement**, on which members and candidates must disclose involvement in civil litigation, criminal investigations, or anything reflecting on their professional conduct
- **Written complaints** from clients, employers, the public, or other members
- **Public sources** — media reports
- **Exam proctor reports** of testing violations
- **Analysis of exam materials** for irregularities

**The process:**
1. Staff **investigate** — requesting a written explanation, interviewing the subject and others, and collecting documents
2. Staff reach a **conclusion**: no sanction, a **cautionary letter**, or proposed **discipline**
3. If discipline is proposed, the member or candidate may **accept** it, or **reject** it and request a **hearing before a DRC panel**

**Sanctions available:**
- Private or public **censure**
- **Suspension** of membership or of participation in the CFA Program
- **Revocation** of membership and of the right to use the CFA designation
- For candidates, **prohibition** from further participation in the programme

**What CFA Institute cannot do:** impose **fines or imprisonment**. Those are matters for regulators and courts. The sanctions are **professional**, and their force derives entirely from the value of the designation and of membership.

</details>

**3.** A scenario describes an analyst with a conflict of interest. Why is 'take no action' almost never the right answer?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Because the Standards are **almost always action-requiring**. Once a scenario establishes that a conflict or concern exists, the Code specifies a **remedy**, and failing to apply it is itself the violation.

**The remedies the Standards specify, and where:**

| Situation | Required action |
| --- | --- |
| A conflict of interest exists | **DISCLOSE** it — fully, fairly, and prominently (VI-A) |
| Additional compensation offered by a client | **PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT** from all parties (IV-B) |
| You possess material non-public information | **ABSTAIN** from trading and from recommending (II-A) |
| A referral fee is involved | **DISCLOSE** it to clients and employer (VI-C) |
| You have concerns about a supervisee's conduct | **INVESTIGATE and escalate** (IV-C) |
| You are asked to violate the law | **Refuse, and dissociate** (I-A) |

**Why candidates are drawn to 'do nothing':** it feels cautious, and in ordinary life avoiding action often avoids error. But the Standards impose **affirmative duties**. Silence in the face of a disclosable conflict is a breach of VI(A), not a neutral choice.

**The other near-universally wrong answer is 'it was legal'** — the Code sets a higher bar than the law (LM1).

**The complementary trap:** assuming **disclosure always cures**. It does not. Material non-public information requires **abstention** — you cannot cure it by telling the client you have it. Identify the **specific remedy** the Standard requires.

</details>

**4.** How should you approach any Ethics scenario on the exam?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A four-step method, applied consistently:**

**Step 1 — Identify the stakeholders and the duties owed.** Who is affected? A client? The employer? Other market participants? The public? Each carries a different duty, and the **hierarchy is client > employer > self** (LM1).

**Step 2 — Identify which Standard is engaged.** Often more than one. Run through the seven quickly: is this about professionalism (I), the market (II), a client (III), the employer (IV), the quality of the work (V), a conflict (VI), or CFA Institute itself (VII)?

**Step 3 — State what that Standard requires.** The recurring remedies are: **disclose**, obtain **prior written consent**, **abstain**, treat **fairly**, have a **reasonable basis**, keep **records**, or put the **client first**. Identify which one applies — this is where most of the discrimination happens.

**Step 4 — Compare the described conduct to that requirement.** Did they do it? Did they do enough? Was the timing right — prior consent obtained *before*, not after?

**Practical exam points:**
- Questions often ask which Standard is **least likely** violated, or which action is **most** appropriate. Read the stem carefully.
- Watch for **timing** words: 'after', 'subsequently', 'having already' often indicate a breach because consent or disclosure came too late.
- Watch for **partial compliance** — disclosing to the employer but not the client, or disclosing verbally where written consent is required.
- When two answers both seem defensible, prefer the one that **puts the client first** and the one that requires **more** rather than less action.

</details>
