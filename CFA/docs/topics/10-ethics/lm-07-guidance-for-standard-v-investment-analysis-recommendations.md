## Core concepts

### Standard V(A) — Diligence and Reasonable Basis

> **Members and Candidates must: (1) exercise diligence, independence, and thoroughness in analyzing
> investments, making investment recommendations, and taking investment actions; and (2) have a
> REASONABLE AND ADEQUATE BASIS, supported by appropriate research and investigation, for any
> investment analysis, recommendation, or action.**

**What constitutes a reasonable basis depends on the circumstances** — the nature of the investment,
the cost of research relative to the position size, the sophistication of the client, and the
firm's resources. But the obligation is always present.

**Using third-party research.** A member may rely on research produced by others **only after making
reasonable efforts to determine that it is sound**. The tests to apply:

| Test | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Assumptions** | Are they reasonable, and clearly stated? |
| **Rigour** | How thorough was the analysis? |
| **Timeliness** | Is it current? |
| **Objectivity and independence** | Was it issuer-paid? Are there conflicts (Equity LM11)? |
| **Review process** | Does the provider have a documented review and quality control process? |

> **"I relied on the firm's research department" is not a defence** unless the member made reasonable
> efforts to establish that the research was sound. Reliance is permitted; **blind** reliance is not.
>
> The same applies to **quantitative models**: a member using a model must **understand its
> assumptions, inputs, and limitations**. Using a black-box model whose behaviour you cannot explain
> is a V(A) breach.

**Group research reports.** A member may be **disassociated from a report they disagree with** while
still permitting the firm to publish it, provided the conclusion has a reasonable basis and the firm
has an adequate review process. A member is **not** required to have their name removed simply
because their view differs — the question is whether the report has a reasonable and adequate basis.

**External advisers and sub-advisers.** A member selecting an external manager must apply the same
diligence: review the manager's code of ethics, compliance procedures, investment process, personnel,
and performance record — and continue monitoring.

**Recommended procedures:** establish a written policy requiring reasonable and adequate basis;
develop detailed, written guidance for analysts; establish measurable criteria for assessing research
quality; document the basis for each recommendation; and maintain a **file of supporting materials**.

### Standard V(B) — Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients

> **Members and Candidates must: (1) disclose to clients and prospective clients the basic format and
> general principles of the investment processes they use to analyze investments, select securities,
> and construct portfolios and must promptly disclose any changes that might materially affect those
> processes; (2) disclose to clients and prospective clients significant limitations and risks
> associated with the investment process; (3) use reasonable judgment in identifying which factors
> are important to their investment analyses, recommendations, or actions and include those factors
> in communications with clients and prospective clients; and (4) DISTINGUISH BETWEEN FACT AND
> OPINION in the presentation of investment analysis and recommendations.**

**The four obligations, each tested:**

| # | Obligation | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Disclose the investment process** — its basic format and general principles, and any **material changes promptly** | A manager who changes from a fundamental to a quantitative process must tell clients |
| **2** | **Disclose significant limitations and risks** | Illiquidity, leverage, concentration, model risk, capacity constraints |
| **3** | **Identify and communicate the important factors** | Use reasonable judgment about what matters; do not bury the key assumption |
| **4** | **DISTINGUISH FACT FROM OPINION** | A forecast is an opinion and must be labelled as one |

> **Obligation (4) is the one most often tested.** A research report stating "earnings will grow 15%
> next year" presents an **opinion as a fact**. It must be framed as a forecast, with the assumptions
> behind it identified.
>
> This connects directly to **Equity LM11**: the assumptions and risk sections of a research report
> are not optional courtesies — they are how a member complies with V(B).

**What must be communicated:**

- The **process**, including any material change
- **Risks and limitations** — including those of the *process*, not only of the investments
- The **important factors** in the analysis
- **Fact clearly distinguished from opinion**

**Communication takes any form** — written reports, verbal conversations, media appearances, social
media. The Standard applies to all of them. A brief format (a one-line recommendation, a media
soundbite) is acceptable, provided clients are told that **more detailed information is available on
request** and that the brief version does not mislead.

**Recommended procedures:** maintain a **records file** of supporting materials; use standardised
report formats with defined sections for assumptions and risks; have a formal review process for
client communications; and provide a written description of the investment process to every client.

### Standard V(C) — Record Retention

> **Members and Candidates must develop and maintain appropriate records to support their investment
> analyses, recommendations, actions, and other investment-related communications with clients and
> prospective clients.**

**The rules:**

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who owns the records** | The **FIRM**, not the member. A departing member may **not** take them (Standard IV(A)) |
| **Retention period** | Local **regulation governs** where it specifies a period. Where it does not, CFA Institute **recommends a MINIMUM OF SEVEN YEARS** |
| **What must be kept** | Supporting research, models, the basis for recommendations, client communications, meeting notes, and the rationale for investment actions |
| **Form** | Hard copy or electronic |

> **The seven-year recommendation is a specific, examinable number.** It applies **only where local
> regulation does not specify a period** — regulation takes precedence where it exists.

**Why it matters in practice:** the records file is what demonstrates compliance with **V(A)** (that
a reasonable basis existed at the time) and with **V(B)** (that the process and risks were
communicated). Without it, a member cannot show that the analysis was sound — and is left arguing
from memory against a documented complaint.

**Recommended procedures:** maintain records for each recommendation; keep supporting documentation
for all research; retain client communications; and — because the records belong to the firm — ensure
the retention system is a **firm** system rather than an individual's personal files.

## Exam traps

> **Trap 1 — Relying on third-party research without checking it.** Reliance is permitted only after
> **reasonable efforts to establish soundness**. "The research department said so" is not a defence.

> **Trap 2 — Using a model you do not understand.** A member must understand a quantitative model's
> **assumptions, inputs, and limitations**. A black box is a V(A) breach.

> **Trap 3 — Thinking disagreement requires name removal from a group report.** A member need **not**
> have their name removed if the report has a **reasonable and adequate basis** — they may simply be
> disassociated from it.

> **Trap 4 — Presenting opinion as fact.** A forecast is an **opinion**. V(B)(4) requires the
> distinction be made explicit.

> **Trap 5 — Disclosing the investments but not the PROCESS.** V(B)(1) requires disclosure of the
> **investment process** and prompt disclosure of **material changes** to it.

> **Trap 6 — Omitting risks and limitations.** V(B)(2) requires disclosure of the significant
> limitations and risks **of the process**, not only of the securities.

> **Trap 7 — Getting the retention period wrong.** **Local regulation takes precedence.** Where it
> is silent, the CFA Institute recommendation is a **minimum of seven years**.

> **Trap 8 — Thinking records belong to the member.** They belong to the **FIRM**. Taking them on
> departure breaches **IV(A)**.

## Self-check

*Closed book. Commit to an answer before opening the fold.*

**1.** An analyst issues a buy recommendation based entirely on a report from an external research provider she has not evaluated. Is this a violation?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Yes. Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis is breached.**

A member **may** rely on third-party research — that is normal and efficient — but only **after making reasonable efforts to determine that the research is sound**. Reliance is permitted; **blind** reliance is not.

**The tests she should have applied:**
- **Assumptions** — are they reasonable, and are they clearly stated?
- **Rigour** — how thorough is the analysis? Does it address the obvious counterarguments?
- **Timeliness** — is it current, or does it predate material developments?
- **Objectivity and independence** — was it **issuer-paid**? Does the provider have investment banking relationships with the subject (Equity LM11)?
- **Review process** — does the provider have documented quality control?

**What 'reasonable efforts' means in practice:** reviewing the provider's methodology, checking a sample of its conclusions against her own work, understanding its business model and conflicts, and documenting that she did so.

**The same obligation applies to quantitative models.** A member using a model must understand its **assumptions, inputs, and limitations**. A black box whose behaviour cannot be explained fails V(A).

**And to external managers:** selecting a sub-adviser requires the same diligence — reviewing their code of ethics, compliance procedures, process, personnel, and record, with ongoing monitoring.

**The practical protection:** a **records file** documenting the diligence performed, which is also what V(C) requires.

</details>

**2.** A research report states: 'The company's revenue grew 12% last year and will grow 15% next year.' What is wrong with this?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**It presents an OPINION as a FACT, breaching Standard V(B)(4).**

The two halves of the sentence are fundamentally different in nature:
- **'Revenue grew 12% last year'** — a **fact**, verifiable from the financial statements
- **'Will grow 15% next year'** — an **opinion**: a forecast, resting on assumptions that may not hold

Presenting them in the same declarative sentence, with the same grammatical form, **implies both are equally certain**. A reader has no way to see where verifiable fact ends and the analyst's judgement begins.

**What V(B) requires instead:**
- **Frame the forecast as a forecast**: 'we forecast', 'we expect', 'our estimate is'
- **Identify the important factors** behind it (obligation 3) — what must be true for 15% to happen?
- **Disclose the significant risks and limitations** (obligation 2) — what would make it wrong?

**A compliant version:** 'Revenue grew 12% in the last financial year. **We forecast** 15% growth next year, driven by the capacity expansion completing in Q2 and assuming pricing holds at current levels. **Key risks**: a delay in commissioning, or the price competition seen in the second half continuing.'

**The connection to Equity LM11:** this is why the **assumptions** and **risk factors** sections of a research report are not courtesies — they are how a member complies with V(B). A report presenting only the bull case, with no stated assumptions and boilerplate risk language, breaches the Standard regardless of how good the underlying analysis is.

</details>

**3.** How long must a member retain records supporting a recommendation?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Local regulation governs where it specifies a period. Where it does not, CFA Institute recommends a MINIMUM OF SEVEN YEARS.**

This is a specific, examinable number, and the conditional matters: the seven-year recommendation applies **only in the absence of a regulatory requirement**. Where a regulator specifies a period — whether longer or shorter — **the regulation takes precedence** (consistent with Standard I(A)).

**What must be retained:**
- Supporting **research and analysis**
- **Models** and their inputs
- The **basis** for each recommendation and investment action
- **Client communications** — reports, correspondence, meeting notes
- The **rationale** for investment decisions

**Form:** hard copy or electronic; the Standard is indifferent.

**Who owns the records: the FIRM, not the member.** A departing member may **not** take them — doing so breaches **Standard IV(A) Loyalty** (LM6, the in-your-head/on-file principle).

**Why it matters practically:** the records file is the **evidence** that a reasonable basis existed **at the time** the recommendation was made (V(A)) and that the process and risks were communicated (V(B)). Without it, a member facing a complaint years later is arguing from memory against a documented allegation — and the absence of records is itself taken as evidence of inadequate process.

**The firm-level implication:** retention must be a **firm system**, not an individual's personal files, precisely because the records belong to the firm and must survive the member's departure.

</details>

**4.** A member strongly disagrees with the conclusion of a team research report that carries her name. What are her options?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**She may be disassociated from the report, but she is NOT required to have her name removed if the report has a reasonable and adequate basis.**

**Standard V(A)** requires a **reasonable and adequate basis** for a recommendation. It does **not** require that every team member agree with the conclusion.

**Why disagreement alone is not a violation:** investment research involves judgement, and reasonable analysts routinely reach different conclusions from the same facts (Equity LM11 — the same DCF structure produces very different values from different assumptions). A firm's report represents the **firm's** view, arrived at through its process. A dissenting member does not make that view unreasonable.

**The test is whether the report has a reasonable and adequate basis**, not whether she personally agrees.

**Her options:**
1. **Accept the firm's view** and allow her name to remain, if the report is soundly researched and the process is adequate — even though she would have concluded differently
2. **Ask to be disassociated** from the report — her name removed — if she is uncomfortable being associated with it
3. **Document her dissent internally**, which is good practice regardless

**When she MUST act:** if the report **lacks a reasonable and adequate basis** — if the research is inadequate, the assumptions unsupportable, or the conclusion not defensible — then **V(A) is being breached** and she must:
- Raise it with the team and with supervisors
- Have her name **removed**
- **Dissociate** from the report (Standard I(A))
- If it proceeds and is misleading, consider whether **I(C) Misrepresentation** is also engaged

**The distinction: disagreement is permissible; association with a report that has no reasonable basis is not.**

</details>
