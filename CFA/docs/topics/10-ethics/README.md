# Ethical and Professional Standards

*The largest topic on the exam, the designated tie-breaker, and the one that cannot be crammed.*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Exam weight** | 15-20% — roughly 27–36 of 180 questions |
| **Study block** | Month 1-5 (parallel), days 1–150 |
| **Budget** | 150 days · 150 hours |
| **Modules** | 10 |
| **Position in study order** | 10 of 10 |

## Why this topic sits here

Ethics runs **in parallel with every other topic, one hour a day, from day 1 to day 150.** That is
the single most important design decision in this plan, and it rests on three facts:

**It is the largest single topic at 15–20%** — more than FSA, Equity, or Fixed Income individually,
and roughly 27–36 questions of the 180.

**It is the official tie-breaker.** Candidates whose total score falls near the minimum passing score
have their **Ethics performance** used to decide the outcome. For a borderline candidate, Ethics is
worth more than its weight.

**And crucially, it does not reward cramming.** Ethics is tested through **scenarios**, not recall.
You are never asked to recite Standard III(B) — you are asked whether a described action violates
it. That is **pattern recognition**, built by repeated exposure to many different fact patterns
**over time**. One hour a day for 150 days beats a 150-hour block, by a wide margin, and the
research on spaced retrieval says so unambiguously.

**The one rule that holds even on a bad day: never cut the Ethics hour.** If a work crunch costs you
three days, take it out of Derivatives. The Ethics hour holds.

> **Study these modules in the calendar order below, not all at once.** LM1 on day 1; LM10 in the
> final fortnight. The whole point of the parallel track is the **spacing** — compressing it defeats
> the design.

## The formulas this topic lives on

There is nothing to calculate. What must be automatic is the **remedy map** — which Standard requires
which action.

```
ABSTAIN (disclosure does NOT cure)    → II(A) MNPI · II(B) manipulation
PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT                 → IV(B) additional compensation · IV(A) independent practice
DISCLOSE                              → VI(A) conflicts · VI(C) referral fees
                                         · I(B) client gifts · V(B) investment process
PRIORITISE (disclosure does NOT cure) → VI(B) clients → employer → self
TREAT FAIRLY                          → III(B)
REASONABLE BASIS                      → V(A)
INVESTIGATE AND RESTRICT              → IV(C)
DISSOCIATE / FOLLOW THE STRICTER      → I(A)
KEEP RECORDS (7 yrs if regulation silent) → V(C)
```

Plus the structure — **4 · 2 · 5 · 3 · 3 · 3 · 2** sub-sections across the seven Standards — and the
hierarchy that resolves most conflicts: **CLIENT > EMPLOYER > SELF**.

## Topic wrap-up

By day 150, before the revision phase:

- [ ] All 10 modules ticked, studied **in calendar order, one hour a day**
- [ ] I can list all seven Standards and every sub-section from memory
- [ ] I can reproduce the **remedy map** without hesitation
- [ ] I can identify every Standard engaged by a multi-issue scenario
- [ ] I know the fifteen highest-value facts in LM10 cold
- [ ] I never answer "do nothing" or "it was legal"
- [ ] I read every stem for **"least likely"** and **"not"**
- [ ] I have completed **300+ Ethics questions** across the 150 days, logged in `trackers/weak-areas.md`
- [ ] My misses are categorised as **recognition** failures or **remedy** failures, and I have
      addressed whichever dominates

**The final check.** Ethics is the highest-expected-return hour in the plan: the largest topic, the
tie-breaker, and the one where the marginal hour improves your score most. If you have done the
parallel track honestly, you will walk into the exam with 150 hours of spaced, scenario-based
practice behind you — which is exactly what the format rewards.
