## Core concepts

### The final 15 hours: application, not new content

Everything in this module is **application of LM1–LM9**. There is no new material. The 15 hours are
for working through **cases** — which is how Ethics is actually tested.

> **Work backwards from the scenario, not forwards from the Standard.** The exam does not ask "what
> does III(B) require?" It describes a situation and asks whether conduct was appropriate. The skill
> is **recognising which Standard a described fact pattern engages** — and that only comes from
> volume.

### The method, applied to every case

| Step | Question | Common answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Who is the client?** | Not always the person paying. Pension → **beneficiaries** |
| **2** | **Which Standard is engaged?** | Often two or three at once |
| **3** | **What remedy does that Standard require?** | Disclose / prior written consent / abstain / prioritise / reasonable basis / record |
| **4** | **Did the conduct meet it — fully, and in time?** | Timing and completeness are where most breaches sit |

### The remedy map — which Standard requires what

**This is the single most useful table in the Ethics topic. If you can reproduce it, most scenarios
resolve quickly.**

| Remedy required | Standards |
| --- | --- |
| **ABSTAIN** (disclosure does not cure) | **II(A)** material non-public information · **II(B)** market manipulation |
| **PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT** | **IV(B)** additional compensation · **IV(A)** independent practice competing with the employer |
| **DISCLOSE** | **VI(A)** conflicts · **VI(C)** referral fees · **I(B)** gifts from clients (to the employer) · **V(B)** the investment process |
| **PRIORITISE** (disclosure does not cure) | **VI(B)** clients → employer → self |
| **TREAT FAIRLY** | **III(B)** dissemination and trade allocation |
| **HAVE A REASONABLE BASIS** | **V(A)** |
| **INVESTIGATE AND RESTRICT** | **IV(C)** supervisory red flags |
| **DISSOCIATE** | **I(A)** from ongoing violations |
| **KEEP RECORDS** | **V(C)** — 7 years where regulation is silent |
| **FOLLOW THE STRICTER** | **I(A)** law vs the Code |

### The fifteen highest-value facts

Drawn from LM3–LM9, these recur across cases more than anything else:

1. **Follow the STRICTER** of law or the Code. Where law is absent or weaker, **follow the Code** — I(A)
2. **Dissociation is required; whistleblowing is not** — I(A)
3. **Gifts from a CLIENT: disclose to the EMPLOYER. From anyone else: token only** — I(B)
4. **Plagiarism includes uncredited IDEAS and second-hand sources.** Recognised factual sources need
   no attribution; **analysis and opinion always do** — I(C)
5. **Personal bankruptcy alone is not misconduct**; bankruptcy involving fraud is — I(D)
6. **Material non-public information → ABSTAIN.** Disclosure does not cure it. **Mosaic theory** is
   permitted — II(A)
7. **Manipulation requires INTENT to mislead.** Large trades are not manipulation — II(B)
8. **In a pension, the client is the BENEFICIARIES**, not the sponsor — III(A)
9. **"Fairly" does not mean "equally."** Different service levels are fine; all clients must have a
   **fair opportunity to act** — III(B)
10. **Managing to a stated mandate: stay within it**, however attractive the alternative — III(C)
11. **Include terminated accounts** in performance — III(D)
12. **Confidentiality has THREE exceptions only**: illegal activity, required by law, client
    permission. It covers **former and prospective** clients — III(E)
13. **Leaving an employer: take what is in your head, not what is on file.** No soliciting **before**
    departure — IV(A)
14. **Reliance on third-party research requires reasonable efforts to establish soundness** — V(A)
15. **"CFA" is an ADJECTIVE.** "John Smith, CFA" ✓ · "John Smith is a CFA" ✗ — VII(B)

### Scenario patterns that recur

| Pattern | Standards usually engaged |
| --- | --- |
| Analyst receives a tip about a pending deal | **II(A)**, and **I(A)** if others are trading |
| Manager leaves to start a competing firm | **IV(A)**, **V(C)** (records), possibly **III(E)** |
| Client offers a performance bonus | **IV(B)** (consent), **I(B)** (independence), **III(B)** (fair dealing) |
| Research note sent to large clients first | **III(B)**, and **VI(B)** if the firm traded |
| Analyst sits on a covered company's board | **VI(A)**, **IV(B)**, **I(B)**, **II(A)** |
| Manager buys outside the fund's stated mandate | **III(C)(2)** |
| Supervisor dismisses a tip about a subordinate | **IV(C)** |
| Report presents a forecast as a fact | **V(B)(4)**, possibly **I(C)** |
| Composite excludes terminated accounts | **III(D)**, **I(C)** |
| Member uses a model they do not understand | **V(A)** |

### Common exam traps, consolidated

> **"Do nothing" is almost never correct.** The Standards impose **affirmative duties**.

> **"It was legal" is never sufficient.** The Code sets a higher bar — I(A).

> **Disclosure does not always cure.** **II(A) requires abstention**; **IV(B) requires prior written
> consent**; **VI(B) requires correct ordering**. Identify the specific remedy.

> **Timing matters.** Consent must be **prior**. Referral fees must be disclosed **before** any
> agreement. Soliciting clients must be **after** departure.

> **Read the question stem.** "Least likely violated", "most appropriate", and "best course of
> action" reward different answers. Ethics questions are frequently phrased negatively.

> **Where two answers seem defensible**, prefer the one that puts the **client first** and the one
> that requires **more** action rather than less.

### How to use the remaining time

**The 15 hours in this module should be spent almost entirely on questions**, not on re-reading.

| Activity | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Re-read LM3–LM9 traps sections, once | 2 hours |
| **Work through 150+ Ethics scenario questions** | 10 hours |
| **Review every miss** — identify which Standard you failed to recognise, and why | 3 hours |

> **The single most useful diagnostic:** for each question you get wrong, ask whether you
> (a) **failed to recognise which Standard applied**, or (b) **knew the Standard but applied the
> wrong remedy**. These are different failures with different fixes. (a) means re-reading the
> Standard's scope; (b) means re-learning the remedy map above.
>

## Formulas to know cold

```
THE FOUR-STEP METHOD
  1. WHO IS THE CLIENT?  (pension → BENEFICIARIES, not the sponsor)
  2. WHICH STANDARD is engaged?  (often two or three)
  3. WHAT REMEDY does it require?
  4. DID THE CONDUCT MEET IT — fully, and IN TIME?

THE REMEDY MAP — the most useful table in Ethics
  ABSTAIN (disclosure does NOT cure)   → II(A) MNPI · II(B) manipulation
  PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT                → IV(B) additional compensation · IV(A) independent practice
  DISCLOSE                             → VI(A) conflicts · VI(C) referral fees
                                          · I(B) client gifts (to employer) · V(B) process
  PRIORITISE (disclosure does NOT cure)→ VI(B) clients → employer → self
  TREAT FAIRLY                         → III(B) dissemination and allocation
  REASONABLE BASIS                     → V(A)
  INVESTIGATE AND RESTRICT             → IV(C) supervisory red flags
  DISSOCIATE                           → I(A)
  KEEP RECORDS (7 years if regulation silent) → V(C)
  FOLLOW THE STRICTER                  → I(A)

THE CONSOLIDATED TRAPS
  "Do nothing" is almost never correct — the Standards impose AFFIRMATIVE duties.
  "It was legal" is never sufficient — the Code sets a HIGHER bar.
  Disclosure does NOT always cure — identify the SPECIFIC remedy.
  TIMING matters — consent PRIOR, referral disclosure BEFORE, soliciting AFTER departure.
  Read the stem — "LEAST likely violated" reverses the answer.
  When torn: prefer CLIENT FIRST and MORE action.
```

## Exam traps

> **Trap 1 — Choosing "do nothing".** The Standards impose **affirmative duties**: disclose, consent,
> abstain, investigate, dissociate, record.

> **Trap 2 — Accepting "it was legal".** The Code sets a **higher** bar than the law — I(A).

> **Trap 3 — Assuming disclosure always cures.** **II(A) requires abstention. IV(B) requires prior
> written consent. VI(B) requires correct ordering.** Use the remedy map.

> **Trap 4 — Missing the timing element.** Consent must be **prior**. Referral disclosure must come
> **before** any agreement. Client solicitation must come **after** departure.

> **Trap 5 — Misreading a negatively phrased stem.** "**Least** likely violated" and "which is
> **not** a breach" reverse the answer. Ethics questions are frequently phrased this way.

> **Trap 6 — Identifying only one Standard.** Most real scenarios engage **two or three**. A question
> asking for the "most appropriate" action may require satisfying several at once.

> **Trap 7 — Misidentifying the client.** The person who **pays** is not always the client — the
> pension case is the standard test.

> **Trap 8 — Studying Ethics by re-reading.** It is tested through **scenarios**. Volume of practice
> questions is the only thing that builds the recognition skill.

## Self-check

*Closed book. Commit to an answer before opening the fold.*

**1.** A portfolio manager at a firm managing a corporate pension plan is told by the plan sponsor's CFO, over dinner paid for by the CFO, that the company will announce a major restructuring next week. The manager sells the company's shares from a different client's portfolio the next morning. Identify every Standard engaged.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information — the primary violation.** The restructuring announcement is **material** (it will move the price) and **nonpublic** (not yet disseminated). The manager **acted on it** by selling. Selling is 'acting' just as buying is — the direction is irrelevant.

**What he should have done:** not traded, not recommended, not tipped; made reasonable efforts to have the issuer **disclose publicly**; and informed **compliance**, who would place the security on the **restricted list**. **Disclosure does not cure this** — abstention was required.

**Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity.** The dinner paid for by the CFO is a benefit from a party with an interest in the manager's conduct. Whether it was modest is a question of degree, but accepting hospitality from a plan sponsor executive and then receiving inside information from him is precisely the pattern I(B) exists to prevent.

**Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care.** He traded in a **different client's** portfolio. He must consider whether that sale was in **that client's** interest, or whether he used one client relationship to act on information obtained through another.

**Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law.** Trading on material non-public information is illegal in most jurisdictions. He has participated in a violation, and must dissociate.

**Possibly Standard IV(C)** if he has supervisory responsibility, and **VI(B)** if any personal or firm account benefited.

**The lesson: a single fact pattern typically engages three or four Standards.** Exam questions often ask which is **least likely** engaged.

</details>

**2.** Reproduce the remedy map: which Standards require abstention, which require prior written consent, and which require disclosure?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**ABSTAIN — disclosure does NOT cure:**
- **II(A) Material Nonpublic Information** — do not trade, recommend, or tip. Telling the client you have inside information does not permit you to act on it
- **II(B) Market Manipulation** — do not engage in it at all

**PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT:**
- **IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements** — written, from **all parties**, **before** accepting
- **IV(A) Loyalty** — independent practice competing with the employer requires written consent from **both** the employer and the persons for whom the work is done

**DISCLOSE:**
- **VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts** — prominently, in plain language, effectively
- **VI(C) Referral Fees** — to employer, clients, and prospective clients, **before** any agreement, in **both directions**
- **I(B) Independence and Objectivity** — gifts from a **client** disclosed to the **employer**
- **V(B) Communication with Clients** — the investment process, its risks and limitations, and material changes

**PRIORITISE — disclosure does NOT cure:**
- **VI(B) Priority of Transactions** — clients → employer → self

**OTHER REMEDIES:**
- **III(B)** — treat fairly (simultaneous dissemination, pro rata allocation)
- **V(A)** — have a reasonable and adequate basis
- **IV(C)** — investigate and restrict on a supervisory red flag
- **I(A)** — dissociate from violations; follow the stricter of law or Code
- **V(C)** — keep records (7 years where regulation is silent)

**The key discrimination: identify whether the Standard is a DISCLOSURE Standard, a CONSENT Standard, an ABSTENTION Standard, or a PRIORITY Standard.** Applying the wrong remedy is the most common Ethics error.

</details>

**3.** An analyst leaves Firm A for Firm B. She takes no files but, from memory, contacts fifteen former clients three weeks after starting at Firm B, using contact details from a public professional directory. She also uses a valuation framework she developed at Firm A, rebuilt from scratch. Any violations?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**No violations — on these facts.** Each element falls on the permitted side of the line.

**(1) Contacting former clients AFTER departure — PERMITTED.** Standard IV(A) prohibits **soliciting clients while still employed**, which would harm the employer and use her position to compete with the firm paying her. Once her employment has **ended**, that duty largely ends and she may compete freely.

**(2) Using publicly available contact information — PERMITTED.** She did not take a client list. A public professional directory is available to anyone. Had she taken Firm A's client records — names, contact details, holdings, or account information — that would be **misappropriation** of the employer's property.

**(3) Rebuilding the valuation framework from memory — PERMITTED.** This is the core principle: **you may take what is IN YOUR HEAD; you may not take what is ON FILE.** Her **skills, knowledge, and experience** are hers. The **files, models, and spreadsheets** belong to Firm A. Rebuilding from scratch uses the former, not the latter.

**The one thing that could change the answer: a non-compete or non-solicitation agreement.** If she signed one, she may be contractually restricted from contacting former clients for a specified period. That is a matter of **contract law**, enforced by courts — not of the Standards — but she would be bound by it nonetheless.

**What WOULD have been violations:** copying the model files, taking the client list, contacting clients before her resignation took effect, or using confidential client information obtained at Firm A (III(E) covers **former** clients).

</details>

**4.** How should you spend the final 15 hours of the Ethics track?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Almost entirely on questions, not on re-reading.**

| Activity | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Re-read the **traps** sections of LM3–LM9, once | 2 hours |
| **Work 150+ Ethics scenario questions** | 10 hours |
| **Review every miss** properly | 3 hours |

**Why questions rather than reading:** Ethics is tested through **scenarios**, not recall. You are never asked 'what does III(B) require?' — you are given a fact pattern and asked whether the conduct was appropriate. The skill is **recognising which Standard a described situation engages**, and that comes only from **volume of exposure to different fact patterns**.

Re-reading the Standards builds **familiarity**, which feels like knowledge and is not. Answering scenarios builds **recognition**, which is what the exam measures.

**The diagnostic to apply to every miss — this is the highest-value part:**

For each question wrong, determine whether you:
- **(a) failed to RECOGNISE which Standard applied**, or
- **(b) knew the Standard but applied the WRONG REMEDY**

These are different failures with different fixes:
- **(a)** means re-reading that Standard's **scope** — what situations it covers
- **(b)** means re-learning the **remedy map** — disclose vs consent vs abstain vs prioritise

Most candidates find their misses cluster heavily in one category, which tells them exactly where the remaining work is. This is the **K/A/C/T** categorisation from `docs/00-orientation/study-method.md` applied to Ethics.

**And remember why this matters disproportionately:** Ethics is the **largest single topic at 15–20%** and the **designated tie-breaker** for borderline candidates. Hours spent here have the highest expected return of any hours in the plan.

</details>
