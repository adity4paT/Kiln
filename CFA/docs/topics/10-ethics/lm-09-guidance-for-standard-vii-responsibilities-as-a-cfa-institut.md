## Core concepts

### Standard VII(A) — Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs

> **Members and Candidates must not engage in any conduct that compromises the reputation or
> integrity of CFA Institute or the CFA designation or the integrity, validity, or security of CFA
> Institute programs.**

**What is prohibited:**

| Prohibited | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Cheating** on the exam, or assisting others to cheat | |
| **Disclosing exam content** | Revealing specific questions, formulas tested, or topics covered — **whether or not you sat that exam** |
| **Violating testing policies** | Continuing to write after time is called; bringing prohibited materials |
| **Misrepresenting information** on the Professional Conduct Statement or a membership application | |
| **Improperly using an association with CFA Institute** to further personal or professional goals | |
| **Misrepresenting CFA Institute policies** or the CFA Program | |

> **The exam content rule catches candidates out.** Discussing **specific questions or exam content**
> after sitting is a violation — including in online forums, study groups, and social media. The
> exam's validity depends on future candidates facing the same conditions.
>
> **What IS permitted:** expressing an **opinion** about CFA Institute, its policies, or the
> programme — including criticism. A member may say the exam was too long, the curriculum is
> outdated, or the policies are wrong. Opinion is protected; **disclosing content** is not.

**Candidates are bound by this Standard from registration.**

### Standard VII(B) — Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program

> **When referring to CFA Institute, CFA Institute membership, the CFA designation, or candidacy in
> the CFA Program, Members and Candidates must not misrepresent or exaggerate the meaning or
> implications of membership in CFA Institute, holding the CFA designation, or candidacy in the CFA
> Program.**

**Maintaining membership** requires **both**:

1. Remitting the **annual dues**
2. Submitting a completed **Professional Conduct Statement** annually

> **A member who fails either is no longer a member and MUST STOP using the designation.** This is a
> mechanical requirement with no grace or discretion, and it is tested directly.

**Referring to candidacy — the rules:**

| Permitted | Not permitted |
| --- | --- |
| "I am a **candidate** in the CFA Program" | "CFA Level I" or "CFA Level II" as a **title or designation** |
| "I passed **Level I** of the CFA Program" | Implying **partial designation** — there is no such thing |
| "I am a Level II **candidate** in the CFA Program" | Using any abbreviation for candidacy |
| Stating you are **enrolled** or **registered** for the next exam | Claiming candidacy when **not currently registered** |

> **You are a candidate only if you are currently REGISTERED for the next scheduled exam.** Having
> passed Level I but not registered for Level II means you are **not** a candidate and may not
> describe yourself as one.

**Using the designation itself:**

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **"CFA" and "Chartered Financial Analyst" are ADJECTIVES, never nouns** | ✓ "She is a **CFA charterholder**." ✗ "She is a CFA." |
| **No pluralising or possessive forms** | ✗ "CFAs", ✗ "CFA's" |
| **The marks must not be more prominent than the member's name** | No larger type, no bold when the name is not |
| **Use either "CFA" or "Chartered Financial Analyst"**, consistently | |
| **No claim of superiority** | Holding the charter does not make a member a better analyst than someone without it, and saying so is a violation |

> **The adjective rule is the single most mechanically tested point in the whole Ethics topic.**
> "John Smith, CFA" is correct. "John Smith is a CFA" is a violation. The designation describes the
> person; it is not a thing the person is.

**Claims about what the designation means:**

- **Permitted:** describing the rigour of the programme, the ethical commitment it requires, and the
  body of knowledge it covers
- **Not permitted:** claiming or implying that holding the charter produces **superior performance**,
  guarantees competence in every area, or makes the holder a better investor than others

> **This follows from Standard I(C) Misrepresentation.** A claim that CFA charterholders outperform
> is an assertion about investment results that cannot be substantiated.

## Exam traps

> **Trap 1 — Using CFA as a noun.** **"John Smith, CFA" is correct. "John Smith is a CFA" is a
> violation.** The marks are **adjectives**. This is the most mechanically tested point in Ethics.

> **Trap 2 — Pluralising or using the possessive.** **"CFAs"** and **"CFA's"** are both violations.

> **Trap 3 — Claiming candidacy when not registered.** You are a candidate **only if currently
> registered** for the next scheduled exam. Having passed Level I is not candidacy.

> **Trap 4 — Using "CFA Level I" as a title.** There is **no partial designation**. Say "I passed
> Level I of the CFA Program" or "I am a Level II candidate".

> **Trap 5 — Forgetting that membership requires BOTH dues AND the Professional Conduct Statement.**
> Failing either means you are no longer a member and **must stop using the designation**.

> **Trap 6 — Thinking criticism of CFA Institute is prohibited.** Expressing an **opinion**,
> including criticism, is **permitted**. **Disclosing exam content** is not.

> **Trap 7 — Discussing specific exam questions afterwards.** A violation, including in study groups
> and online forums.

> **Trap 8 — Claiming superior performance.** Implying the charter produces better investment results
> breaches VII(B) and **I(C) Misrepresentation**.

## Self-check

*Closed book. Commit to an answer before opening the fold.*

**1.** Which of these are permissible? (a) 'Jane Doe, CFA'  (b) 'Jane Doe is a CFA'  (c) 'Jane Doe, Chartered Financial Analyst'  (d) 'Our team includes three CFAs'  (e) 'Jane Doe, CFA Level II candidate'

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 'Jane Doe, CFA' — PERMITTED.** The correct form. The designation follows the name as an adjective modifying it.

**(b) 'Jane Doe is a CFA' — VIOLATION.** Uses CFA as a **noun**. The marks are **adjectives**, never nouns. Correct: 'Jane Doe is a **CFA charterholder**.'

**(c) 'Jane Doe, Chartered Financial Analyst' — PERMITTED.** The full form is equally acceptable. Use either the abbreviation or the full form consistently.

**(d) 'Our team includes three CFAs' — VIOLATION.** **Pluralises** the mark, and uses it as a noun. Correct: 'Our team includes three **CFA charterholders**.'

**(e) 'Jane Doe, CFA Level II candidate' — VIOLATION as written.** It places the mark in a position implying a **partial designation**, which does not exist. Correct: **'Jane Doe is a Level II candidate in the CFA Program'** — and only if she is **currently registered** for the Level II exam.

**The rule that resolves all five: CFA and Chartered Financial Analyst are ADJECTIVES.** They modify a noun (charterholder, candidate, member); they are never the noun themselves. Test any phrasing by asking whether the word could be replaced by an ordinary adjective and still read correctly — 'she is a tall' fails, and so does 'she is a CFA'.

</details>

**2.** A member forgets to pay her annual dues and does not submit her Professional Conduct Statement. May she continue to use the designation?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**No. She must stop using the designation immediately.**

Maintaining CFA Institute membership requires **both**:
1. **Remitting the annual dues**
2. **Submitting a completed Professional Conduct Statement** annually

Failing **either** means she is **no longer a member**, and a person who is not a member **may not use the designation**. There is no grace period and no discretion in the Standard — it is mechanical.

**Continuing to use it would breach:**
- **Standard VII(B)** — misrepresenting membership status
- **Standard I(C) Misrepresentation** — holding out a credential she does not currently hold

**What she must do:**
- **Stop using the designation** on business cards, email signatures, marketing materials, LinkedIn, and anywhere else, **immediately**
- **Remedy the lapse** — pay the dues and submit the Professional Conduct Statement
- **Resume use only once membership is reinstated**

**Why the Professional Conduct Statement matters so much:** it is the annual self-disclosure on which the **Professional Conduct Program** depends (LM2). Members disclose involvement in civil litigation, criminal investigations, and anything reflecting on their professional conduct. Without it, CFA Institute has no mechanism to learn of matters that should trigger an inquiry. Failing to submit it is therefore not a clerical oversight — it defeats the self-regulation the designation rests on.

</details>

**3.** A candidate posts on a forum after the exam: 'The exam was far too long for the time allowed, and I thought Question 43 on convertible bonds was ambiguous.' Which part is a violation?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**The first half is permitted. The second half is a violation.**

**'The exam was far too long for the time allowed' — PERMITTED.** This is an **opinion** about the exam and CFA Institute's policies. Members and candidates are **free to express opinions**, including criticism, about CFA Institute, the CFA Program, the curriculum, and the examination process. **VII(A)** does not require deference.

**'Question 43 on convertible bonds was ambiguous' — VIOLATION of Standard VII(A).** This **discloses specific exam content**: the question number and its subject matter. It compromises the **integrity, validity, and security** of the programme.

**Why it matters:** the exam's validity depends on every candidate facing the same conditions. Disclosing which topics appeared, which formulas were tested, or the content of specific questions gives future candidates — or candidates sitting in a later window — an unfair advantage, and undermines the credential for everyone who holds it.

**The line: OPINION is protected; CONTENT is not.**

**What is also prohibited under VII(A):**
- Discussing exam content in **study groups**, private messages, or any forum
- Disclosing content **whether or not you sat that exam** — passing on what someone else told you is equally a violation
- Continuing to write after time is called, or bringing prohibited materials
- Misrepresenting information on the Professional Conduct Statement

**Note:** candidates are bound by the Standards **from registration**, so this applies before anyone becomes a member.

</details>

**4.** A firm's marketing brochure states: 'Our portfolio managers hold the CFA charter, the gold standard in the industry, which means they deliver superior investment results.' What is wrong?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**The final clause violates Standard VII(B) and Standard I(C) Misrepresentation.**

**'Which means they deliver superior investment results' — VIOLATION.** Holding the CFA charter demonstrates that a person has **passed a rigorous examination programme**, has the required **professional experience**, and has committed to the **Code and Standards**. It does **not** demonstrate — and cannot demonstrate — that they will produce superior investment performance.

The claim is an **assertion about investment results that cannot be substantiated**, which breaches **I(C) Misrepresentation** as well as VII(B)'s prohibition on **exaggerating the meaning or implications** of holding the designation.

**'The gold standard in the industry' — borderline, and probably acceptable.** Describing the programme's rigour and standing is permitted. But it edges toward a superiority claim, and prudent marketing would rephrase it.

**What the firm MAY legitimately say:**
- 'Our portfolio managers hold the CFA charter, which requires passing three rigorous examinations covering a comprehensive body of investment knowledge.'
- 'Our CFA charterholders have committed to the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct.'
- 'The CFA Program requires four years of qualified professional experience in addition to the examinations.'

**The distinction: describing what the designation REQUIRES and REPRESENTS is permitted. Claiming what it PRODUCES in investment outcomes is not.**

**Also check the presentation:** VII(B) requires that the marks not be **more prominent** than the member's name — so the brochure's typography matters too.

</details>
