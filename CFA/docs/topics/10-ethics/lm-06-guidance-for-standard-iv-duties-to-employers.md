## Core concepts

### Standard IV(A) — Loyalty

> **In matters related to their employment, Members and Candidates must act for the benefit of their
> employer and not deprive their employer of the advantage of their skills and abilities, divulge
> confidential information, or otherwise cause harm to their employer.**

**Remember the hierarchy: CLIENT > EMPLOYER > SELF.** The duty to the employer is real but ranks
**below** the duty to clients.

**Independent practice:** a member must **not undertake independent practice that could result in
compensation or other benefit in competition with their employer** unless they obtain **written
consent** from both their employer and the persons for whom they undertake the work.

> Note the distinction: **preparing** to leave (finding office space, registering a company) is
> generally permissible; **competing** while still employed is not.

**Leaving an employer — the rules that are tested:**

| Permitted after departure | Prohibited |
| --- | --- |
| **Skills, knowledge, and experience** acquired at the firm | Taking **client lists**, records, or files |
| **Memory** of client relationships and public information | Taking **research reports, models, or other work product** |
| **Soliciting clients using PUBLICLY available information** | **Misappropriating** trade secrets or confidential information |
| | **Soliciting clients before departure** |
| | Any conduct causing harm to the former employer while still employed |

> **The principle: you may take what is IN YOUR HEAD; you may not take what is ON FILE.** Knowledge
> and skill belong to the member; records, models, and client lists belong to the employer.
>
> **Once the resignation is effective**, the duty largely ends — a departed employee may compete
> freely and may contact former clients **using publicly available information** — unless bound by a
> separate **non-compete or non-solicitation agreement**, which is a matter of contract law rather
> than of the Standards.

**Whistleblowing:** a member may act contrary to the employer's interest **only if the duty to
protect the integrity of capital markets, to protect clients, or to comply with the law requires
it**. Personal gain or a desire to damage the employer is never a justification.

**Social media:** communications that could be construed as professional should follow the same
standards as any other professional communication. Client information must not be shared, and
connections built using employer resources may belong to the employer.

### Standard IV(B) — Additional Compensation Arrangements

> **Members and Candidates must not accept gifts, benefits, compensation, or consideration that
> competes with or might reasonably be expected to create a conflict of interest with their
> employer's interest unless they obtain WRITTEN CONSENT from all parties involved.**

**The requirement is specific and the wording matters:**

- **WRITTEN consent** — not verbal
- From **ALL parties involved** — the employer **and** the client or other party offering the benefit
- **BEFORE** accepting — prior consent, not subsequent notification

**What is covered:** performance bonuses offered by a client, gifts beyond token value, outside
consulting fees, a directorship carrying remuneration, and any benefit that could bias the member
toward the payer.

> **Connection to Standard I(B):** a gift from a client must be **disclosed to the employer** under
> I(B). If it constitutes **compensation** — a bonus for performance, or an ongoing arrangement —
> **IV(B) additionally requires prior WRITTEN CONSENT**. Exam scenarios often test the escalation
> from disclosure to consent.

**Recommended procedure:** make an **immediate written report** to the employer detailing any
proposed additional compensation, the parties involved, the nature of the benefit, and its duration,
and obtain written consent before accepting.

### Standard IV(C) — Responsibilities of Supervisors

> **Members and Candidates must make reasonable efforts to ensure that anyone subject to their
> supervision or authority complies with applicable laws, rules, regulations, and the Code and
> Standards.**

**Who is a supervisor?** Anyone with **supervisory authority** over others, whether or not they have
a formal management title, and **including compliance officers**. A member who delegates a task
retains supervisory responsibility for it.

**What "reasonable efforts" requires:**

| Obligation | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Understand the firm's compliance system** and ensure it is adequate |
| **Decline supervisory responsibility** in writing until an adequate system is in place, if one does not exist |
| **Respond to indications of wrongdoing** — investigate promptly and thoroughly |
| **Take action** — place limits on the individual's activities pending the investigation and impose appropriate consequences |
| **Not rely on hearsay** — conduct an actual investigation |

> **Two failures are tested repeatedly:**
>
> **(1) Inadequate response to a red flag.** Relying on the employee's assurance, or on a superficial
> review, is **not** a reasonable effort. The supervisor must **investigate**, and must **restrict
> the employee's activities while the investigation proceeds**.
>
> **(2) Accepting supervisory responsibility without adequate systems.** If the firm has no
> functioning compliance procedures, the member must **decline the supervisory role in writing**
> until they are established. Accepting it and then pointing to the absence of systems as a defence
> is not available.

**A supervisor is not automatically liable for every violation by a subordinate.** The Standard
requires **reasonable efforts**, not a guarantee. A supervisor who has established adequate
procedures, enforced them, and responded properly to indications of wrongdoing has discharged the
duty even if a violation occurred.

**Recommended procedures for the compliance system itself:**

- A written **code of ethics** and compliance procedures, distributed to all staff
- **Designate a compliance officer** with clear authority and independence
- **Training** on the Code and Standards
- Procedures for **detecting** violations, not only for prohibiting them
- **Periodic review** of the procedures' effectiveness
- Clear **consequences** for violations, consistently applied
- A route for staff to **raise concerns safely**

## Formulas to know cold

```
IV(A) LOYALTY TO EMPLOYER  (but remember: CLIENT > EMPLOYER > SELF)
  Independent practice competing with the employer requires WRITTEN CONSENT
  from BOTH the employer and the persons for whom the work is done.

  LEAVING AN EMPLOYER — the governing principle:
    YOU MAY TAKE WHAT IS IN YOUR HEAD. YOU MAY NOT TAKE WHAT IS ON FILE.
    PERMITTED: skills, knowledge, experience, memory; soliciting clients after departure
               using PUBLICLY AVAILABLE information
    PROHIBITED: client lists · records and files · research reports and models
                · trade secrets · SOLICITING CLIENTS BEFORE DEPARTURE

  Non-compete agreements are a matter of CONTRACT LAW, not of the Standards.

IV(B) ADDITIONAL COMPENSATION ARRANGEMENTS
  Requires WRITTEN consent, from ALL PARTIES, obtained BEFORE accepting.
  Covers: client performance bonuses · gifts beyond token value · outside consulting
          · remunerated directorships

  ESCALATION FROM I(B): a gift from a client must be DISCLOSED to the employer under I(B);
  if it is COMPENSATION, IV(B) additionally requires PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT.

IV(C) RESPONSIBILITIES OF SUPERVISORS
  "Reasonable efforts" to ensure those under supervision comply.
  Applies to anyone with SUPERVISORY AUTHORITY, including COMPLIANCE OFFICERS.
  Delegation does NOT transfer responsibility.

  ON A RED FLAG: INVESTIGATE promptly and thoroughly · RESTRICT the employee's
  activities pending the investigation · do NOT rely on assurances or hearsay

  IF THE FIRM HAS NO ADEQUATE COMPLIANCE SYSTEM:
    DECLINE SUPERVISORY RESPONSIBILITY IN WRITING until one is in place.

  A supervisor is NOT automatically liable — the test is REASONABLE EFFORTS, not a guarantee.
```

## Exam traps

> **Trap 1 — Thinking a departing employee may take nothing.** They may take **skills, knowledge,
> experience, and memory**, and may solicit former clients **after departure** using **publicly
> available** information. What they may not take is **records, files, models, and client lists**.

> **Trap 2 — Missing that soliciting clients BEFORE departure is prohibited.** Preparing to leave is
> permissible; competing while still employed is not.

> **Trap 3 — Accepting verbal consent under IV(B).** **WRITTEN** consent, from **ALL parties**, is
> required — and **before** accepting, not after.

> **Trap 4 — Confusing I(B) disclosure with IV(B) consent.** A gift from a client requires
> **disclosure** to the employer (I(B)); **compensation** requires **prior written consent** (IV(B)).

> **Trap 5 — Thinking only titled managers are supervisors.** **Anyone with supervisory authority**
> is covered, **including compliance officers**. Delegation does not transfer responsibility.

> **Trap 6 — Accepting an employee's assurance as an adequate response to a red flag.** The
> supervisor must **investigate** and **restrict the employee's activities** while doing so.

> **Trap 7 — Blaming the absence of compliance systems.** A member must **decline supervisory
> responsibility in writing** until adequate systems exist. Accepting the role and then citing their
> absence is not a defence.

> **Trap 8 — Assuming a supervisor is automatically liable.** The test is **reasonable efforts**. A
> supervisor who established and enforced adequate procedures has discharged the duty.

## Self-check

*Closed book. Commit to an answer before opening the fold.*

**1.** An analyst plans to leave her firm to start a competing business. Before resigning she (a) rents office space, (b) copies her research models to a personal drive, and (c) emails three clients about her new venture. Which are violations?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Renting office space — NOT a violation.** **Preparing** to leave is permissible. A member may make arrangements — lease premises, register a company, obtain financing, engage professional advisers — provided these preparations do not **harm the employer** or involve using the employer's resources or confidential information.

**(b) Copying research models — VIOLATION of Standard IV(A).** The models are the **employer's property**, created on the employer's time with the employer's resources. Taking them **misappropriates the employer's work product**.

**The governing principle: you may take what is IN YOUR HEAD; you may not take what is ON FILE.** She may use the **skills and knowledge** she gained building those models to build new ones at her new firm. She may not take the files.

**(c) Emailing clients before resigning — VIOLATION of Standard IV(A).** **Soliciting clients while still employed** directly harms the employer and deprives it of the advantage of her skills and abilities — she is using her position and access to compete with the firm paying her.

**What she may do AFTER her resignation takes effect:** contact former clients **using publicly available information** (a public directory, a professional network she did not build with employer resources), and compete freely.

**The exception:** a separate **non-compete or non-solicitation agreement** may restrict her further. That is a matter of **contract law**, enforced by courts, not of the Standards — but she is bound by it nonetheless.

</details>

**2.** A client offers a portfolio manager a bonus of 2% of any return above 10%, payable directly to the manager. What must the manager do?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Obtain WRITTEN consent from ALL parties BEFORE accepting.** Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements applies.

**Why it is caught:** the arrangement creates a **conflict with the employer's interest**. The manager now has a direct personal incentive to:
- **Favour this client** over others in the firm — devoting more time, better ideas, and potentially better trade allocations, which breaches **III(B) Fair Dealing**
- **Take excessive risk** in this account to reach the 10% threshold, breaching **III(C) Suitability**
- Act on the **personal payoff** rather than on the employer's compensation structure

**The precise requirements — each element is tested:**
- **WRITTEN** consent, not verbal
- From **ALL parties involved** — both the **employer** and the **client**
- Obtained **BEFORE** accepting, not disclosed afterward

**The recommended procedure:** make an **immediate written report** to the employer specifying the parties, the nature and amount of the compensation, and its duration, and obtain written consent before any arrangement takes effect.

**The escalation from Standard I(B):** a **token gift** from a client requires only **disclosure to the employer** under I(B). This is not a token gift — it is an **ongoing compensation arrangement**, which triggers the stricter IV(B) requirement of **prior written consent from all parties**.

**If consent is refused:** the manager must decline the arrangement.

</details>

**3.** A compliance officer receives an anonymous tip that a trader is front-running client orders. He asks the trader, who denies it, and takes no further action. Has he breached Standard IV(C)?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Yes. This is an inadequate response to a red flag.**

**Standard IV(C)** requires supervisors to make **reasonable efforts** to ensure that those subject to their supervision or authority comply with laws and the Code. A **compliance officer** is explicitly covered — supervisory responsibility does not require a management title.

**Why asking and accepting the denial is insufficient:**
- Someone engaged in front-running will **deny it**. The denial carries essentially no information
- It is not an **investigation** — no trading records were examined, no timing analysis performed, no pattern review undertaken
- The trader was **left free to continue** the alleged conduct while the matter was unresolved

**What a reasonable effort requires:**
1. **Investigate promptly and thoroughly** — review the trader's personal and client trading records, compare timestamps of personal trades against client order entry, look for a pattern
2. **Restrict the trader's activities** pending the investigation — supervise or suspend personal trading, and impose additional review of order handling
3. **Escalate** to senior management and, where appropriate, obtain external or legal input
4. **Document** the investigation and its findings
5. **Take appropriate action** if the conduct is substantiated — discipline, and reporting to regulators if required

**Note what is NOT required:** the supervisor is **not automatically liable** for the trader's misconduct. The test is **reasonable efforts**, not a guarantee. A supervisor who investigated properly and responded appropriately has discharged the duty even if the violation occurred.

**The breach here is the response, not the underlying conduct.**

**The underlying conduct, if proven,** would breach **VI(B) Priority of Transactions** and **III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care**.

</details>

**4.** A member is promoted to head of a new department at a firm with no written compliance procedures. What should she do?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**Decline the supervisory responsibility IN WRITING until adequate compliance procedures are established.**

**Standard IV(C)** requires supervisors to make reasonable efforts to ensure compliance by those under their authority. **Without an adequate compliance system, that is impossible** — there is no framework to enforce, no documented standards for staff to follow, and no mechanism to detect violations.

**What she must do:**
1. **Inform senior management in writing** that adequate compliance procedures do not exist
2. **Recommend their adoption**, specifying what is needed
3. **Decline supervisory responsibility in writing** until they are in place
4. **Retain a copy** of the correspondence

**Why writing matters:** it creates the record that she identified the deficiency and refused the role. Verbal objection leaves her exposed — if a violation later occurs, the firm's account of events may differ from hers.

**What an adequate system looks like:**
- A written **code of ethics** and compliance procedures, distributed to all staff
- A designated **compliance officer** with authority and independence
- **Training** on the Code and Standards
- Procedures for **detecting** violations, not merely prohibiting them
- **Periodic review** of the procedures' effectiveness
- Clear, consistently applied **consequences**
- A safe route for staff to **raise concerns**

**The trap being tested:** accepting the role and later pointing to the absence of systems as a defence is **not available**. By accepting supervisory authority the member assumes the obligation. **Declining in writing is the required response.**

</details>
